entry 65 — where is MC-ESO's +16.6-point excess over the volume-proportional null made?

Question (queue 1, from entry 64): the excess is in the restart DRAW distribution
(fixed repel radius 0.02*span = 0.195 too small to push a draw out of a wide basin)
or in POST-LANDING budget residency (hunts burning evaluations in basins already
reached)?  Pre-registered rejection: draws within +-3 points of the null AND
pts-per-arrival comparable between MC-ESO and NM-Restart => neither, a third component.

Both named candidates are refuted.  The third component is the draw->landing descent.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(i) DRAWS  —  N09-Vincent3D, 400,000 evals (full budget), 15 seeds, 2 arms
    scripts/reseed_to_landing.py --csv  (traces every _diversified_reseed return
    value and the endpoint of the hunt it started; both mapped to the nearest true
    optimum, same Voronoi rule as the entry-64 null).  30 runs, 4 parallel, 17 min.

                     widest-quartile share      distinct cells (of 216)
    arm              draws     landings         drawn near    landed in
    base             0.814     0.964            205           63
    commit_place     0.808     0.937            165           81
    null (uniform)   0.814        --            --            --

    base:         draws vs null   p = 0.679, 7/15 seeds above  => indistinguishable
                  landings vs draws (paired, within seed) p = 6.1e-05, 15/15 seeds,
                  median gap +0.151
    commit_place: draws vs null   p = 0.847, 6/15 seeds above
                  landings vs draws p = 6.1e-05, 15/15 seeds, median gap +0.127

    => The restart draw is volume-proportional to three decimals.  The fixed repel
       radius is NOT the culprit.  The whole +0.150 is made between draw and landing.
    => The clearest form: the draws touch 205 of 216 basins, the landings 63.
       MC-ESO's restart mechanism already visits essentially the whole landscape;
       the population descent then discards two thirds of it.
    => commit_place draws into FEWER cells (165, because 19 of its 20 slots are
       clones of one anchor) yet lands in MORE (81).  Coverage is not lost at the draw.

    Mechanism, from the geometry: sigma_init = 0.2*span = 1.950 against a
    nearest-neighbour spacing of 0.291 (narrowest quartile) to 0.546 (q75 boundary).
    A population dropped on a narrow-basin draw and scattered at sigma 1.95 spans
    3.6x-6.7x the local basin spacing, and the greedy mu+lambda then keeps whichever
    member fell in the wider basin.  commit_place fixes only the PLACEMENT
    (0.1*d_local around one anchor) and leaves run sigma at base -- it recovers
    0.027 of the 0.150 (18%), consistent with entry 64's 1.9 of 16.6 (11%) measured
    on arrivals.  The remaining 0.123 is run sigma, which commit_place does not touch.

    Estimator direction (do not over-read): `narrow_draw_wide_land` (hunts landing in
    the widest quartile although none of their draws was in it) is 0/660 for base and
    77/641 for commit_place.  This is NOT evidence about base -- with 20 independent
    draws per hunt and q4 holding 81.4% of the volume, the chance that all 20 miss q4
    is 0.186^20 ~ 0, so the statistic is structurally near zero for any per-slot-draw
    arm.  It is informative only for commit_place, whose 20 slots are one draw.
    The evidence about base is the paired share comparison above, not this counter.
    Same reason the `match` rate (0.634 base / 0.307 commit_place) is generous.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(ii) RESIDENCY  —  recomputed from analysis/hm/e64/n09_visits.csv.gz, NO new runs
     (scripts/hunt_coverage.py --analyze, columns pts/arr and q4 pts% added here).
     Full table in residency.txt.  eps = 1e-5, N09, 15 seeds:

     method          cov%    q4 arr%    pts/arr    q4 pts%
     MC-ESO          27.3      98.0        2.6       97.2
     MC-ESO-place    35.2      96.1        2.6       94.9
     NM-Restart      64.8      83.0       42.9       83.1
     NMMSO           98.6      41.9        1.0       41.9

    => Residency does not explain the excess, and the sign is the wrong way round:
       NM-Restart consumes 42.9 evaluations per entry into an eps-ball against
       MC-ESO's 2.6 (16x more lingering, p = 3.4e-06, A12 = 0.00) and is nonetheless
       15 points LESS concentrated and covers 2.4x more.  For residency to be the
       cause, MC-ESO would have to be the one lingering.
    => Residency is width-neutral in every arm: q4 pts% == q4 arr% to within
       1.2 points in all four (97.2/98.0, 94.9/96.1, 83.1/83.0, 41.9/41.9).
       Weighting basins by budget spent instead of by entries adds no concentration,
       so there is no "budget sink in the wide basins" on top of the arrival bias.
    => MC-ESO vs MC-ESO-place: pts/arr identical (3.86 vs 3.86, p = 0.62) while the
       q4 shares differ (p = 1e-05).  The within-method manipulation moves the
       concentration without moving residency at all -- they are separate axes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Verdict against the pre-registered conditions

  draws within +-3 points of the null           YES (delta -0.000, p = 0.679)
  pts/arrival comparable MC-ESO vs NM-Restart   NO (2.6 vs 42.9) -- but the
      difference refutes residency rather than supporting it, and q4 pts% == q4 arr%
      shows residency carries no width bias in any arm.

  => Both named candidates are eliminated.  The third component is named and located:
     the descent from the restart draw to the hunt endpoint, driven by a restart
     sigma (0.2*span) several times the local basin spacing.
